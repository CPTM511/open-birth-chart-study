# Research questions and completion gates

## The original claim

The motivating claim says that charts for many people born in the 1990s/2000s contain no life or luck periods across parts of ages 20–50 or 20–60, sometimes interpreted as impending mass death. No single authenticated source, population denominator, canonical four-system rule or exact falsifiable wording has been established for that claim in this project. R8 therefore tests a disclosed operational question, not every possible retelling of the rumor.

## What R8 asks

For each eligible birth record, can four versioned calculation workflows produce forty annual structural indices at completed ages 20–59? Are years above a within-person relative threshold aligned across at least three workflows more or less often than under a specified independent-phase reference? How does this differ by source and age window? How sensitive are these outputs to declared clock and coordinate stress points?

The 2026–2070 display includes only years when a record is actually within the specified age window. A zero in a computed structural index is not missing life. A blank outside the age window is not a death. A software domain limit is not a lifespan prediction.

## What would count as completion

| Layer | R8 acceptance condition | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| Birth acquisition | Actual downloads, input schema, provenance, cleaning counts, immutable hashes | A representative world sample |
| Four calculation workflows | Named conventions, source-linked code, complete defined annual trajectories, implementation checks | Every traditional school or full classical interpretation |
| Structural comparison | Fixed scalar/normalization/reference, all six tests, corrected p values, missingness and stress results | Accuracy against real events |
| Reproducibility | Public code and aggregates, versioned dependencies, source reconstruction scripts, recorded verification scope | Independent expert approval or a guaranteed future download |
| Public release | Public repository URL and verified remote contents | Substack publication until separately posted |

## Remaining research

1. Find additional public individual birth frames with credible clocks, especially outside Brazil and among Chinese and other Asian 1990s/2000s births. More records from an already overrepresented source do not repair geographic selection.
2. Resolve competing historical calendrical and interpretive conventions before calculating their results. Preserve disagreements as separate model versions.
3. Extend the presently bounded Bazi/Ziwei interpretation clauses and Qizheng degree-dependent methods, retaining prerequisites, rescue conditions and unknowns. A practitioner should be able to reject an implementation on an explicit counterexample.
4. Test a frozen rule against independently withheld or future observable outcomes if that research is later resumed. Do not interpret the structural analysis as a substitute.

There is no promised million-person global birth-clock dataset in this release. Millions of source rows inspected and thousands of computed birth records are different quantities.
